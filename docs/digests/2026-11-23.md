- **In plain words**: Genetic studies can find spots in our DNA linked to diseases or traits, but those spots act inside particular cell types. This paper describes a software tool, EPIC2, that checks which cell types are most likely involved in a trait by comparing the trait-linked DNA spots with maps of which DNA regions are open and active in each cell type. The text describes the method and where the code is available; it does not report specific results.
- **Why it matters**: Pinpointing the right cell type is a key step in turning genetic findings about diseases into biological understanding. The text does not show how well EPIC2 performs compared with other tools, so its practical value is not yet established from this description alone.
- **Background needed**: - GWAS (genome-wide association study): a scan of many people's genomes to find DNA variants (SNPs, single-letter changes) associated with a trait or disease.
- Summary statistics: the per-variant association results from a GWAS, used without needing individual genetic data.
- Chromatin accessibility: which parts of DNA are physically open and available to be switched on; measured per cell in single-cell assays.
- Single-cell data: measurements taken cell by cell, so different cell types can be compared.
- Linkage disequilibrium (LD): nearby DNA variants tend to be inherited together, so their statistical signals are correlated and must be handled carefully.
- P-value: a number indicating how surprising a result would be if there were no real effect; smaller means stronger evidence.
- Cauchy combination test: a method for merging several P-values into one. · level: research-level

The resulting P-values are combined using the Cauchy combination test. - **In plain words**: Cells must move to heal wounds, and cancer cells must move to spread. This paper built a fast screening test to measure how well many normal and cancer cell lines from different organs migrate and invade. - Serum and calcium: components of standard lab growth media; "low-nutrient" media here means less serum, less calcium and near-normal blood sugar levels.

<details><summary>Original abstract</summary>

Phenotypic and metabolic screens presented here could identify prospective therapeutics not only for cancer metastasis, but also diseases driven by the dysregulation of wound-healing/regeneration.

### [Metabolic priming recruits tumour cells across a mitochondrial vulnerability threshold and sensitizes them to chemotherapy](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.13.766410v1)
*Iborra, F. J.* · `cancer biology` · bioRxiv doi:10.64898/2026.11.13.766410 v1 · relevance 0.04

- **In one sentence**: Single-cell study using TMRM fluorescence excursions under pulsed light as a readout of mitochondrial vulnerability to photodynamic damage, showing that metabolic priming (TEPP46, ethyl pyruvate, camptothecin) shifts cells into a responsive state, with combination effects reported as descriptive and needing replication.
- **In plain words**: Some cancer cells survive treatment because a tumour is made of varied cells, and only some are in a fragile state at any moment. This paper asks whether drugs can push more of those cells into a fragile mitochondrial (energy-producing structure) state, and then tracks individual cells with a fluorescent dye that is also a light-activated damaging agent. The authors report that a cell's own resting dye level and its change over time can predict whether it dies, and that several priming treatments increase the share of responding cells.
- **Why it matters**: It offers a way to watch single cells' vulnerability over time rather than only at one endpoint, and it suggests a possible timing window for combining drugs with photodynamic treatment. The work is a proof of concept in cultured cells and human tumour sections only, with no in vivo testing, so its relevance to real treatment is uncertain.
- **Background needed**: - Intratumour heterogeneity: cells within one tumour differ, so some survive therapy.
- Mitochondria: cell structures that produce energy; their membrane voltage drives dye uptake.
- TMRM: a fluorescent dye that accumulates in mitochondria depending on membrane voltage; here it also acts as a light-activated photosensitizer that generates reactive oxygen species (damaging oxygen molecules).
- Photodynamic treatment: killing cells with light plus a light-activated agent.
- PKM2 and TEPP46: an enzyme involved in energy metabolism and a small molecule that activates it.
- Camptothecin and TRAIL: an anticancer drug and a cell-death signalling protein.
- Bliss independence: a standard baseline for expected combined effect of two treatments; survival below it suggests synergy.
- AUC (area under the ROC curve): a 0 to 1 score of how well a measure predicts outcome; about 0.5 is chance. · level: research-level

<details><summary>Original abstract</summary>

Intratumour heterogeneity allows a fraction of cancer cells to escape otherwise effective treatment. Here we ask whether metabolic priming can recruit heterogeneous tumour cells into a transient mitochondrial state that is selectively vulnerable to photodynamic damage. Tetramethylrhodamine methyl ester (TMRM) has previously been shown to act as a mitochondria targeted photosensitizer; we exploit its voltage-dependent accumulation and dynamic fluorescence during pulsed illumination to quantify vulnerability in single cells. The response is a reproducible fluorescence excursion, a rise to a maximum followed by a decay, accompanied by reactive oxygen species production. Comparing seventeen descriptors of this excursion against subsequent fate in 195 cells across four arms shows that quantities referenced to each cell's own resting value outperform static intensity. The simple difference Fmax F0 predicts death with held-out areas under the curve of approximately 0.75 to 0.79, whereas resting intensity alone carries little transferable information and time to peak does not discriminate fate in the datasets tested. Metabolic priming with the PKM2 activator TEPP46 or with ethyl pyruvate increases the fraction of responding cells; under TEPP46 almost the whole population enters the responding state. PKM2 abundance is associated with faster entry into that state, and PKM2 is heterogeneous in human skin tumour sections. Four hours of camptothecin shifts the response distribution, increasing the fraction of cells above the untreated median from 50% to 76%, identifying a candidate intervention window. In combination experiments, TMRM photodynamic treatment produced survival below the Bliss-independence expectation with camptothecin or TRAIL, in both treatment orders and across three cell lines; these combination measurements are descriptive and require independent replication. The study therefore identifies a perturbation-revealed mitochondrial vulnerability that can be pharmacologically recruited and followed dynamically at single-cell resolution. It is a proof of concept in cultured cells and human tumour sections and has not been tested in vivo.
