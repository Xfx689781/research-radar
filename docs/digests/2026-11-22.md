<details><summary>Original abstract</summary>

Arm separation is an important technical tool in the study of critical and near-critical planar percolation systems. When combined with RSW estimates, it proves quasi-multiplicativity of arm-event probabilities, links the derivatives of different events and allows the identification of universal arm exponents. <details><summary>Original abstract</summary>

We prove dynamical edge universality for three families of interacting particle systems for every $β>0$: $β$-Dyson Brownian motion with an analytic potential, weighted $β$-corners processes, and a class of discrete-time Markov chains. ### [Conditional propagation of chaos for systems of particles with polynomial interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26022v1)
*Vlad Bally, Eva Löcherbach* · `math.PR` · arXiv:2611.26022v1 · relevance 0.14

- **In one sentence**: We consider N interacting particles taking values in the real numbers and evolving in discrete time with time step 1/n. (first sentence of the abstract; AI summaries are off)

<details><summary>Original abstract</summary>

We consider N interacting particles taking values in the real numbers and evolving in discrete time with time step 1/n. Each particle follows an individual dynamics, driven by its own noise, which depends on the total configuration via a polynomial interaction. Moreover, all particles receive the same collective perturbation. This perturbation is given by a multilinear homogeneous sum of order $ m \geq 2$ of independent centered random fields which are evaluated at the current positions of the particles. We show that, as N and n tend to infinity, the system converges to the solution of a conditional McKean-Vlasov equation which is driven by an additional Brownian motion which emerges in the limit from the collective perturbations. Our proof combines the invariance principle of Mossel, O'Donnell and Oleszkiewicz (2010), the CLT for Wiener chaoses in the Wasserstein-2-form of Ledoux, Nourdin and Peccati (2015) and a conditional law of large numbers for weighted U-statistics proven in this paper.

<details><summary>Original abstract</summary>

In statistical applications, often more than one hypothesis is of interest and, thus, multiple testing problems occur naturally. To control the family-wise error rate, popular adjustment methods, like the Bonferroni and Holm correction, have been developed. However, these methods do not use the dependence structure of the test statistics. Consequently, they are conservative in most settings. To increase the power of the tests, the joint dependence structure of the test statistics can be incorporated for the construction of critical values, for example, by using resampling schemes as bootstrap procedures to approximate the joint distribution. <details><summary>Original abstract</summary>

We study estimation of the mean latent composition in a bivariate Poisson mixture. Conditional on an unobserved intensity S and proportion U, two counts are independent Poisson variables with respective means SU and S(1-U). The mixing law is unrestricted on the Cartesian product [a,b] x [0,1], where a and b are finite constants satisfying 0 < a < b, and dependence between S and U is allowed. Although the mixing distribution is identifiable, the usual ratio of mean counts estimates an intensity-weighted proportion and may be biased for theta = E[U]. Simulations illustrate the bias-variance trade-off, the role of polynomial regularization, and the distinction between theoretically guaranteed and empirically calibrated adaptation.
